Records of journeys that now exist only in photographs and in my memory. Though those journeys may have tormented me when they were real, now that time has distorted them and brought them to an end, they remain as memories of freedom—a freedom I long to return to. Every day, I take out those memories and revisit them.

이제는 사진과 내 기억 속에만 존재하는 여행의 기록들. 그 순간에는 여행의 아름다움을 느끼기보다 낯선 곳에서 무사히 하루하루를 살아내는 일이 더 버겁게 느껴졌을지도 모른다. 그러나 시간은 여과지처럼 힘겨웠던 순간들을 걸러내고 좋은 기억만을 남겼다. 그렇게 남은 것은 내가 다시 돌아가고 싶은 자유의 기억. 나는 매일 그 기억을 펼쳐 다시 들여다본다.

When I was a kid, I had a terminal case of Hongdae syndrome. I listened to Nirvana and the Beatles on a CD player—now a Y2K relic—tuned in to Bae Chul-soo’s Music Camp, and sang Kim Kwang-seok at karaoke, convinced I was special. I wasn’t. I was ordinary—worse, a loser. I’m mostly cured now. It only flares up once or twice a year, in the standing section when an international act comes to Korea. For a few hours, I get smug about the setlist—“They’re playing this?” “They skipped that?”—and become the special person with special taste my younger self dreamed of. A smug little archive for fellow occasional sufferers.
